# The problems we're solving

## The big picture

Choosing a school has become a maze: public, charter, private and microschools, plus vouchers,
scholarships, tutoring and after-school programs. Today's search tools ask parents to filter by
location, grade and test scores, and assume they already know what to look for.

Families with money and time lean on friends and advisors. Families without them face an
information gap, and they lose the most. GreatSchools already has the data. The problem is that a
parent has to know the right words and filters to reach it.

**Our goal:** move parents from a one-time search to a guided conversation where they learn what
matters, and end with schools they want to visit.

## How we chose

GreatSchools shared ten problems parents face. We ranked them and focused on the top two. The next
three are handled along the way.

## 1. Parents don't know what to ask

**The problem.** Parents type a few words and miss needs they never think to mention. GreatSchools
told us mental health is now in parents' top five concerns, yet parents rarely ask about it.

**What we built.**
- The agent asks one question at a time until it knows the family: how the child learns, getting
  there and pickup, feeling safe and supported, classes, interests and budget.
- It spots trade-offs the parent wouldn't see: "This school's care ends at 5, but a free library
  club runs until 6:30. Would that work?"
- It suggests needs the parent didn't mention, such as counseling support.
- It keeps going after a save or a comparison, so the conversation doesn't stop at a list.

## 2. Parents don't know about help with cost

**The problem.** A private school over budget gets ruled out, even when a scholarship would cover
about half. Families with less money rule out options without knowing help exists.

**What we built.**
- Private schools show the cost after a typical Florida scholarship: "$16,500 a year · about
  $8,300 with a typical scholarship". The figure comes from the state's published average award
  ($8,200 in 2024-25), labeled as an estimate.
- The comparison table and the budget question use the same figure.
- Local partners in the visit planner can help with scholarship forms.

## 3. Parents don't know the jargon

**The problem.** Terms like IEP, 504, CTE and "summary rating" stop parents before they start.

**What we built.** Every label, question and answer uses everyday words ("extra help with
learning", "help learning English"). A term only appears if the parent uses it, and then it's
explained. Terms that still appear, like "Charter" or "STEM", have a dashed underline: hover or
tap to see a one-line meaning. Parents can also ask "What is a charter school?" and get a short
answer with a source.

## 4. Parents have no time to research

**The problem.** Working parents can't check every school's hours, transport and costs.

**What we built.** One sentence replaces the filters. The agent checks every school against every
need in seconds, shows the best four first, and finds programs that fill a school's gaps.

## 5. Parents can't tell what to trust

**The problem.** A single rating hides what's behind it, and old or missing data looks the same as
good data.

**What we built.** Every need on every school is marked confirmed, partly met or needs a closer
look, with the evidence. Old or odd data is flagged instead of hidden. "How I checked this" shows
the steps the agent really took.

## One more: the journey ends at "Saved"

GreatSchools told us local nonprofits and parent guides visit schools and know them well, but most
parents don't know they exist. So after saving or comparing, the app offers a visit with a local
partner. The journey now ends with a parent seeing a school in person, with someone who knows it.
